## Persona

**Laura Mendoza — fictional / synthetic profile.** Laura is a municipal water-utility operations supervisor who manages a small repair-dispatch team. She works from mixed reports, has limited crews and pressure to close tickets, and knows spreadsheets and ticket trackers. She is skeptical of AI recommendations, needs to explain priority choices, and is concerned about politicized equity weights, overstated verification, and extra operational work.

## Spreadsheet Comparison

- AquaRail adds deterministic baseline/equity score comparison, a rule-based bias review flag, missing-owner/next-action/stale checks, explicit status-transition gates, and a blocked-verification counter.
- A spreadsheet or basic tracker can show owner, next action, dates, status, formulas, and override notes with less navigation. It could also surface unresolved tickets with filters if maintained well.
- AquaRail makes unresolved conditions visible in event rows and counts events with any unresolved flag, but a spreadsheet can identify them just as easily with reliable columns and filters. AquaRail does not provide assignment notifications, escalation, or real dispatch integration.
- The explicit state machine has meaningful value because it prevents direct suspected-to-verified transitions and requires evidence fields. Its current limits are that evidence is operator-declared and not authenticated.
- Override history records previous/new rank, reason, and time, making a correction more structured than an informal spreadsheet edit. It lacks operator identity and durable shared audit storage.
- Bias testing can reveal a deliberately unfair ordering, but its threshold and weights remain demo heuristics; without explanation/validation it can become another opaque score.
- It is not supported to claim AquaRail is generally faster or superior. Laura might voluntarily use it for a short adversarial review before dispatch, but likely would not use this prototype as her primary live ticket tracker without integration and a less fragmented flow.

## Worst Confusion

The most consequential confusion is whether ticking physical-confirmation, repair, and post-repair fields means evidence was actually inspected or authenticated. Under pressure to close tickets, a declaration could be mistaken for proof, weakening the distinction between repaired and outcome verified. The evidence gate is valuable, but it only validates entered booleans.

## Fix Applied

Added a short disclosure beside the evidence fields: “Esta demo no adjunta ni autentica pruebas. Los campos registran declaraciones; la verificación requiere revisión humana de evidencia fuera de esta herramienta.” The existing human evidence gate, status transitions, and editable controls are unchanged.

## Re-test

Source walkthrough after the change: the evidence section now labels the inputs as declarations and states that the tool neither attaches nor authenticates proof, while requiring human review outside the tool. The state machine still requires `repaired` plus physical confirmation, repair evidence, post-repair verification, and loss-reduced fields; a failed attempt remains blocked and counted. The UI regression test asserts the disclosure is rendered. This re-test is source/test based; no browser session or screenshot was available.

## Remaining Confusions

- The baseline heading says “Solo pérdida” even though feasibility contributes to its score.
- Rank changes lack a short event-specific explanation in the rendered UI.
- The bias threshold and vulnerability weights are demo choices whose legitimacy requires local review.
- Staleness does not show elapsed time, and an administrative edit resets the last-updated clock.
- Evidence is still self-entered, with no attachment, identity, or independent authentication.
- Browser-local overrides are not a durable shared audit trail.
- The prototype adds a review screen and cannot establish that it saves dispatch time compared with a well-maintained tracker.
